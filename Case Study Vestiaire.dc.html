Reducing Friction in Mobile Sign-up

A redesign of Vestiaire Collective’s account creation experience, refined after a design review.

Role
UX & UI Designer
Industry
Luxury resale e-commerce
Duration
2 days + review iteration
Tools
Figma
BeforeAfter

The redesigned screen is live: type a password to see validation respond.

Why I chose this screen

The screen looked clean but asked a lot at once, at the first commitment someone makes to a marketplace.

My goal wasn’t to redesign the interface. It was to remove the friction standing between the user and the marketplace.

The problem

Five things compete for attention before the one task: creating an account.

  • Multiple form fields
  • Password requirements
  • Marketing consent
  • Legal agreement
  • Three social sign-ins

These competing elements may increase the cognitive effort required to complete sign-up.

Pain points

  1. Multiple secondary actions compete with the primary task.
  2. Password rules take up space and stay static as you type.
  3. Marketing consent appears before the user has experienced any value.
  4. Legal copy adds reading effort to a simple task.
Raised in design review
  1. The heading repeated onboarding.
  2. Fields and social buttons felt cramped.
  3. One checkbox mixed terms and marketing.

Final redesign

Design decisions
Competing secondary actionsStrengthen the hierarchy around one primary button.
Password rules demand attentionCompact guidance that responds as you type.
Marketing consent interrupts the taskA separate, optional choice, unticked by default.
Legal copy adds reading effortOne clear statement with accessible links to each document.
Cramped fields and social buttons48px controls, 16px text and padding, 12px spacing.
What changed after review

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

What I learned

Good UX isn’t about adding more. It’s about removing unnecessary effort.

The review sharpened it: reducing friction also means making every step easy to reach and understand.
